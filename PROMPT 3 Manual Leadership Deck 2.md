# PROMPT 3 — Manual Approach · Leadership Briefing

Copy everything below the line into a fresh session.

---

Build me a PowerPoint deck (.pptx) for a senior leadership audience. Title slide plus **exactly four content slides — no more**. Dense, infographic-led, data-carrying. No bullet-point filler.

The deck asks leadership to adopt a tiered partner-enablement model and run it **entirely by hand** for one quarter. Nothing is built. There is no platform, no automation, no AI, no new headcount and no code. **Do not describe, propose, cost or allude to any tooling, automation or AI programme anywhere in this deck** — not as a future phase, not as a comparison, not as a backlog. The subject is the manual operating model and nothing else.

## Context you are working from

I lead work on an enterprise ECM platform team. ECM provides repository services to partner applications for storage, archival and records management, with metadata compliance and document classification applied on arrival. Three scrum teams, thirty-plus engineers, every partner intake delivered through a dedicated capacity model from inception to release.

**The structural problem.** Capacity required = number of concurrent intakes × a fixed engineering depth applied to every intake. Partner demand sets the first term and we do not control it; the second is fixed by our own delivery model. With both outside our control, the only lever is headcount. This is an allocation problem, not a throughput problem.

**Two causes.** (1) *The data deficit* — the requirement for a piece of information is discovered a step at a time, by an engineer, after the intake has landed; each discovery costs days of waiting; the squads estimate **30–40% of total elapsed time**; it is tier-independent, so no re-tiering fixes it, and it is the most draining work the squads do. (2) *Uniform allocation* — of 38 onboarding scenarios assessed against what each actually requires, 8 need no design, 14 need a judgement or two, 14 genuinely need a dedicated pod, 2 are migration campaigns — and **all 38 currently get the same dedicated team**. Without an objective rule, depth is allocated by influence rather than by need.

**The response** is a tiered enablement model: Lean (delivery on a proven pattern with no allocated capacity), Lean+ (shared pool, two mandatory design checkpoints, a named guide, sub-linear scaling), Dedicated (allocated pod, inception to release, linear scaling), plus a Migration Factory for remediation run as campaigns. Tier is assigned by ECM against a published seven-determinant rubric with hard overrides, never chosen by the partner.

## The argument this deck makes

The model does not need to be built to be run. Every mechanism it depends on has a human equivalent available to us today: a written question guide instead of a guided intake, a lead with a scoring sheet instead of a tiering engine, a shared pool with a calendar instead of orchestrated assurance, one maintained tracker instead of a cockpit. The knowledge already sits with the triage leads. What is missing is the operating discipline, the published rule, and the authority to apply it — all three of which are decisions, not engineering.

So the ask is a quarter of disciplined practice at close to zero cost, after which we know from measurement rather than assumption whether the model works, where effort actually concentrates, and what the real tier distribution against live demand looks like. None of those numbers exist today.

### What the manual approach actually is — four changes, nothing built

1. **A deeper triage conversation.** Triage becomes the session where the full requirement is established, not just classified. A pre-read issued to the partner, a 90-minute Discovery Triage worked from a written question guide with the reason explained at the point of asking, a 30-minute internal Tiering Review, and a one-page Triage Outcome Record issued afterwards. This single change is where most of the benefit sits: the clarification cycle is not automated away, it is removed by asking earlier and better.
2. **Triage leads assign the tier and the guardrails.** The lead scores seven determinants on a sheet, applies the hard overrides, assigns the tier outright, selects which guardrails apply to that intake, and records the justification. Calibrated before start, reviewed fortnightly as a group, one appeal per intake within five days which must name the determinant in dispute.
3. **A shared pool drawn from existing squads.** Four to six people — a permanent core of two plus rotating six-to-eight-week secondments — serving Lean and Lean+ with two mandatory checkpoints per intake, published office hours and a weekly escalation duty. No new headcount; the people already exist inside the three squads.
4. **One tracker, maintained by hand.** A spreadsheet or an existing board. Tier scores, open gaps with owners and dates, gate status, clarification cycles counted, stage entry and exit dates, effort by team. This is the measurement instrument and the primary output of the quarter.

**The only thing written** is a Triage Guide: the tier model, the seven determinants and their scoring, the thresholds and overrides, the full 54-point question set with the reason for each item, the completeness bands, the guardrails by tier, the escalation triggers, and four templates. Roughly one week of two experienced engineers plus a governance analyst. Be precise with the audience about the size of that: writing guidance is days of work by people who already hold the knowledge.

### The limits — state them plainly, it is what makes the proposal credible

| Distribution | Lean | Lean+ | Dedicated | Factory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 0 | 0 | 38 | 0 |
| Running the model by hand | 5 | 17 | 14 | 2 |

**Lean+ works well by hand; Lean largely does not.** Lean+ is a human process by design — scheduled design assurance delivered by a shared pool, booked checkpoints, a named contact. People with a calendar can do all of it. Lean assumes the partner can proceed on their own: pick up a proven pattern, provision inside policy bounds, clear their own gates and see their own state. With everything mediated by a person, only the five simplest, most-repeated patterns can be run that way without the lead effectively doing the work for them. **Seventeen of the twenty-two lighter-tier scenarios therefore land in Lean+, and manual Lean stays deliberately narrow.**

**What it delivers:** most of the Lean+ benefit — 17 scenarios move from continuous dedicated allocation to scheduled assurance; a measurable fall in clarification cycles from the deeper triage alone; long-lead items started at triage rather than discovered mid-delivery; the effort and elapsed-time baseline measured for the first time; the real tier distribution against live demand; a calibrated rubric and trained leads; 14 genuinely complex intakes receiving the depth they need without competing against the other 24.

**What it does not deliver:** removal of the clerical load — artefact preparation, routing and tracking all stay manual; scale, because manual effort per intake stays roughly constant as demand grows; consistency independent of people, since two leads will score the same intake differently until calibration takes hold; guardrails that cannot be waived under schedule pressure; a durable asset that survives the people who built it; a broad Lean tier.

### The plan and the evidence review

Twelve weeks: **1–2 Prepare** (write the guide, calibrate the leads, stand up the pool, build the tracker, publish the tier model to partners) · **3–6 Run in parallel** (every intake gets deep triage and a tier assignment but is still delivered under the current model — real data, zero delivery risk, four weeks of scoring practice before assignments bind real work) · **7–10 Operate the tiers** (assignments take effect, Lean+ with named guides and checkpoints, first Lean intakes on the simplest patterns) · **11–12 Evidence review**.

The criteria for that review, agreed in advance: distribution holds (50%+ route to Lean or Lean+); lighter tiers hold (escalation back to Dedicated below 25%); clarification cycles fall measurably against the baseline; partners accept assigned tiering (appeal rate below 20%, most unsuccessful); the baseline exists (effort and elapsed time per tier, per phase); the bottleneck is identified — a quantified statement of where manual effort actually concentrates; no compliance regression on classification, retention or access.

Cost: ~2 weeks preparation · 0 new headcount · 0 lines of code · 1 quarter.

## Design system

- 13.3 × 7.5 inches (`LAYOUT_WIDE`). Margins 0.45" left and right.
- Palette — navy `15304F` (primary, Dedicated), teal `0B6E6E` (Lean), amber `9A6410` (Lean+), plum `5B4A72` (Factory), rust `A44A2A` (accent / alert), green `2E6B4F` (positive), ink `1B2432`, muted `55606E`, slate `7C8798`, line `D4DAE2`, panel `F4F7FA`. Tints: `E6F2F1` / `FAF1DF` / `E8EDF4` / `F1EEF6` / `FBF0EB` / `E9EEF4`.
- Fonts — Cambria for titles and big numbers, Calibri for everything else. Nothing else.
- Every tier keeps the same colour on every slide, without exception.
- Consistent slide header: a small numbered navy square, a 24pt Cambria title, a 10.5pt muted subtitle, a right-aligned section tag, and a hairline rule beneath.
- Title slide: full navy background, a teal vertical bar at the far left edge, kicker in letter-spaced caps, large Cambria title, subtitle, a short teal rule, four metadata columns, and a footer rule with "Draft for review". Keep the title short enough for one line.
- Rounded rectangles (radius ~0.05), soft shadows, hairline borders. No gradients, no accent stripes under titles, no decorative colour bars.

## Build instructions

Use `pptxgenjs` in Node. Set `pres.layout` before adding slides. Hex colours with **no** `#` and no alpha. Build a fresh options object for every `addShape`/`addText` — never share one. Shadow offsets must be ≥ 0. Use `charSpacing`, not `letterSpacing`. Put `isTextBox: true` on every `addText` and `margin: 0` wherever text must align to a shape edge. `rectRadius` only works on `ROUNDED_RECTANGLE`.

Write a small shared helper library first (slide header, stat tile, card, segmented bar, legend, chevron chain, table, note band, callout band) and compose the slides from it.

**Speaker notes are mandatory on every slide** via `slide.addNotes()` — what a presenter would actually say: the argument, the number to point at, the objection to pre-empt, the line to deliver verbatim. Several hundred words per slide. Pre-empt in particular the two objections this proposal attracts: "if it works manually, why would we ever invest?" — answered by the honest limits on slide 3, the constant per-intake effort and the untouched clerical load — and "isn't this just more process?" — answered by the parallel run, which produces evidence at zero delivery risk.

**QA before handing it over:** convert to PDF, render every slide to an image and inspect each one. Check first for text overflowing or cut off at a shape boundary — especially the last wrapped line inside bordered callout boxes, which is the most common failure. Then check for cards colliding with the band below them, content off the slide edge, and large dead gaps. Fix and re-render.

## Tone

Executive register, no hedging. The central discipline is honesty about the limits: do not oversell this. Make clear it reallocates depth rather than removing work, that the clerical load is almost entirely untouched, that per-intake effort does not fall as demand grows, and that manual Lean is deliberately narrow at five scenarios. That honesty is what separates a credible operating proposal from an optimistic one — and it is what earns the right to be believed at the week-12 review.
